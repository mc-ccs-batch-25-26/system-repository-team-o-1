Project Context
The Civil Service Examination (CSE) in the Philippines is one of the most critical professional assessments administered by the Civil Service Commission (CSC). Passing this examination is a prerequisite for permanent employment in government positions, making it a significant milestone for thousands of Filipino aspirants each year. Despite its importance, many examinees struggle to prepare effectively due to the lack of structured, personalized, and engaging study tools available to them.
Traditional review materials for the Civil Service Examination are largely static and one-dimensional. Most commercially available reviewers consist of printed question banks or basic digital platforms that present questions without any adaptive logic, motivational framework, or personalized feedback. These tools provide no mechanism for identifying a user’s specific weaknesses, offering guided improvement paths, or sustaining long-term engagement. As a result, many review sessions become tedious and ineffective, leading to poor retention and low pass rates.
The primary stakeholders affected by this problem are Filipino professionals and fresh graduates who intend to enter government service. These users typically review on their own, with limited access to structured guidance or intelligent feedback. The absence of a web-based, adaptive, and gamified review tool designed specifically for the Professional-level Civil Service Examination represents a clear and unaddressed engineering problem in the local educational technology landscape.

Purpose and Description
CiviQuest is a web-based gamified reviewer designed to help Filipino aspirants prepare for the Professional-level Civil Service Examination. Accessible through desktop browsers, the system guides users through a structured and personalized study experience by combining adaptive quiz delivery, AI-powered explanations, and game mechanics into a single integrated platform.
The primary function of CiviQuest is to replace passive, static reviewing with an active, responsive learning experience. Users begin with a diagnostic pre-test that identifies their strengths and weaknesses across the major examination subject areas. The system then delivers daily adaptive quizzes that prioritize topics where the user needs the most improvement. When a user answers a question incorrectly, the DeepSeek AI API generates a contextual explanation that clarifies the mistake and reinforces correct understanding. An AI chatbox is also available for users to ask follow-up questions without leaving the platform.
To sustain motivation across extended study periods, CiviQuest incorporates a gamification layer that rewards users with experience points (XP), levels, badges, and streaks. A readiness indicator shows users how prepared they are for the actual examination. Progress tracking and statistics give users a clear view of their improvement over time. The platform is built using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript for the front end, with Supabase serving as the cloud-based backend and database, and Visual Studio Code as the development environment.

Objectives and RQ Alignment
The general objective of this study is to design, develop, and evaluate CiviQuest, a gamified web-based application that serves as an adaptive and intelligent reviewer for the Philippine Civil Service Examination. Specifically, this study aims to:

RQ1 – How can user performance be analyzed effectively to identify individual strengths and weaknesses?
Objective: Implement a diagnostic pre-test mechanism that assesses the user’s knowledge across the Civil Service Examination subject categories of Verbal Ability, Quantitative Ability, and Logical Reasoning, and uses the results to establish a personalized performance baseline that drives subsequent content delivery.

RQ2 – How can an adaptive and engaging reviewer be developed to sustain user motivation and improve learning outcomes?
Objective: Design and develop a web-based quiz system that integrates AI-powered explanations via the DeepSeek AI API, personalized question delivery based on weak area prioritization, and gamification mechanics including XP, leveling, achievement badges, and streaks to sustain daily user engagement throughout the review period.

RQ3 – How can the system’s effectiveness be measured in terms of user performance improvement?
Objective: Evaluate the effectiveness of CiviQuest through a comparative study measuring mock Civil Service Examination pass rates between a control group of ten participants who reviewed without the system and an experimental group of ten participants who used CiviQuest as their primary review tool.

Scope and Limitation
CiviQuest is scoped as a web-based application developed using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, with Visual Studio Code as the primary development environment and Supabase as the cloud-based backend and database layer. The system is designed for desktop browser use and targets Professional-level Civil Service Examination candidates. It covers the primary subject categories of the professional examination: Verbal Ability (Grammar and Correct Usage), Quantitative Ability (Mathematics), and Logical Reasoning.
The core features included in the Minimum Viable Product (MVP) are the diagnostic pre-test, daily adaptive quizzes, XP and leveling system, AI-powered wrong answer explanations via DeepSeek AI, AI chatbox, wrong answer review, achievement badges, timed quiz mode, progress tracking and statistics, category-based lessons, and offline question caching through the browser.
The following limitations are acknowledged. First, the system covers only the Professional-level examination; Sub-professional level content is deferred to future development. Second, CiviQuest is designed for desktop browsers only; mobile browser optimization and a dedicated mobile application are outside the current scope. Third, most features require an active internet connection, as quiz delivery, AI explanations, and progress synchronization depend on Supabase and the DeepSeek AI API. Fourth, the validation study uses a sample of twenty participants, which limits the generalizability of findings. Fifth, the AI explanation feature is dependent on the availability and performance of the DeepSeek AI API as a third-party service.

Domain Literature
Domain Problem and Current Practice
Examination preparation tools have historically relied on static content delivery, where learners are given fixed question sets with no mechanism for performance-based adjustment. Research on web-based quiz applications has demonstrated that this approach produces inconsistent learning outcomes, particularly when feedback is limited to simple correct or incorrect responses. Rüth et al. [1] investigated the effects of different feedback types on learning with quiz applications and found that elaborated informational feedback, which explains why an answer is correct, produced better knowledge retention than standard corrective feedback alone. Their limitation, however, was that the study did not incorporate adaptive content delivery, meaning all learners received the same questions regardless of their individual performance patterns. This gap is directly relevant to CiviQuest, where personalized question prioritization based on weak areas is a core design requirement.

Comparable Systems and Prior Implementations
Gamified review platforms such as Quizizz have been studied as comparable implementations. These systems addressed the problem of low learner engagement in self-directed review by introducing timed questions, points, and leaderboard mechanics into the quiz format. Studies on Quizizz reported improvements in student motivation and participation, but noted that correct response rates did not always translate to full conceptual understanding, since the gamification layer was applied without adaptive logic or explanatory feedback [2]. Duolingo, another widely studied platform, demonstrated that streak mechanics, XP, and leveling sustained daily engagement in language learning contexts, but its design is domain-specific and does not generalize to competency-based standardized examinations such as the Civil Service Exam [3]. The shared limitation across these systems is the absence of an integrated approach that combines gamification, adaptive question delivery, and AI-assisted explanation within a single web-based application.

Relevant Algorithms, Frameworks, and Architectural Approaches
Deterding et al. [4] established that meaningful gamification requires challenge, feedback, and a sense of progression rather than superficial reward overlays. Hamari, Koivisto, and Sarsa [5] reviewed empirical studies on gamification and confirmed that positive effects on motivation and engagement are context-dependent and require deliberate design. On the adaptive learning side, a scoping review by du Plooy et al. [6] covering 69 studies found that pre-knowledge assessments were the most common indicator for activating adaptive content delivery, and that 59 percent of reviewed studies reported improved academic performance when personalized adaptive learning was implemented. These findings directly support the CiviQuest design decision to begin with a diagnostic pre-test and use its results to drive subsequent question selection. For the AI component, Winkler and Söllner [7] reviewed chatbot-mediated learning systems and found that immediate, contextual feedback is among the most effective features for improving outcomes in self-directed study environments.

Evaluation Methods Used in Similar Projects
Studies on comparable systems have predominantly used pre-test and post-test designs with control and experimental groups to measure effectiveness. Rüth et al. [1] used an experimental design comparing feedback conditions on knowledge acquisition and retention scores. Studies on adaptive learning platforms similarly employed comparative performance metrics between groups that received adaptive content and those that did not [6]. Usability evaluations using structured questionnaires have also been used as a secondary instrument alongside performance metrics to assess user acceptance. CiviQuest adopts this same methodology: a comparative study with a control group and an experimental group measured by mock examination pass rates, supplemented by a post-study usability questionnaire administered to the experimental group.

Synthesis Matrix
The following matrix summarizes the key sources reviewed for this study, organized by their approach, demonstrated strength, and identified limitation or gap.

Strength: Demonstrated that elaborated explanatory feedback improves knowledge retention over simple correct/incorrect responses. Limitation: Did not incorporate adaptive content delivery; all learners received the same questions regardless of performance.
[2] Mia et al. (2024) – Approach: Assessment of Quizizz gamification features in classroom settings. Strength: Showed improvements in student motivation and participation through points, timers, and leaderboards. Limitation: Gamification was applied without adaptive logic or explanatory feedback, limiting depth of learning outcomes.
[3] Loewen et al. (2019) – Approach: Case study of Duolingo as a mobile-assisted language learning platform using streaks, XP, and leveling. Strength: Demonstrated sustained daily engagement and positive effects on language learning habit formation. Limitation: Domain-specific to language acquisition; mechanics do not generalize to competency-based standardized examinations.
[4] Deterding et al. (2011) – Approach: Conceptual framework defining gamification as the use of game design elements in non-game contexts. Strength: Established foundational design principles distinguishing meaningful gamification from superficial reward overlays. Limitation: Theoretical framework without empirical validation of specific mechanics in examination preparation contexts.
[5] Hamari, Koivisto, and Sarsa (2014) – Approach: Systematic review of empirical studies on gamification effectiveness. Strength: Confirmed positive effects of gamification on motivation and engagement across multiple contexts. Limitation: Effectiveness is highly context-dependent; results vary significantly based on implementation quality and user characteristics.
[6] du Plooy et al. (2024) – Approach: Scoping review of 69 studies on personalized adaptive learning in higher education. Strength: Found that pre-knowledge assessments and adaptive content delivery improved academic performance in 59 percent of reviewed studies. Limitation: Most reviewed platforms are institution-deployed systems; few address self-directed examination review in developing country contexts.
[7] Winkler and Söllner (2018) – Approach: Review of chatbot-mediated learning systems and intelligent tutoring. Strength: Identified immediate, contextual feedback as one of the most effective features for improving outcomes in self-directed study. Limitation: Effectiveness varies based on individual learner characteristics and the quality of AI-generated responses.


02-chapter-2-related-literature / c-technical-gap.md
――――――――――――――――――――
Technical Gap
The literature reviewed in this chapter establishes that gamification, adaptive learning, and AI-assisted feedback are individually effective approaches to improving learner engagement and academic performance. Gamification sustains motivation through reward structures and progression systems [4][5]. Adaptive learning ensures that study effort is directed toward areas of greatest need [6]. AI-powered feedback supports deeper conceptual understanding by explaining mistakes rather than simply marking them [1][7]. However, none of the reviewed systems or studies combine all three of these approaches within a single platform designed for self-directed examination preparation.
Existing Civil Service Examination review tools available on the web are limited to static question banks with basic scoring feedback. They do not implement adaptive question delivery based on learner performance, do not incorporate gamification mechanics beyond simple scoring, and do not provide AI-generated explanations for incorrect answers. Gamified platforms such as Quizizz and Duolingo address engagement but lack the adaptive and AI components [2][3]. Adaptive learning platforms address personalization but are deployed in institutional settings and are not designed for the Philippine CSE context [6].
The specific gap that CiviQuest addresses is the absence of a web-based reviewer for the Philippine Professional-level Civil Service Examination that integrates adaptive quiz delivery, gamification, and AI-assisted explanation within a single accessible and desktop-optimized platform. This gap is supported by the consistent finding across the reviewed sources that no single existing system combines all three of these components for the target examination context. CiviQuest directly responds to this gap by implementing a unified system where the diagnostic pre-test drives adaptive question selection, the gamification layer sustains daily engagement, and the DeepSeek AI API provides contextual explanations that promote understanding over memorization.

Institutional Framework
This study followed the Design Science Research (DSR) framework as its overarching research paradigm. DSR guided the study by framing CiviQuest as a purposeful artifact designed to solve the identified problem of inadequate Civil Service Examination review tools. The development process was organized using the Agile Scrum methodology, which structured work into iterative sprints that allowed the team to progressively build, review, and refine the web application across multiple development cycles. Each sprint produced a functional increment of CiviQuest, and feedback from sprint reviews informed subsequent iterations. The combination of DSR and Scrum ensured that the project maintained both research rigor and practical delivery focus throughout the development period.

Product Backlog
The CiviQuest product backlog was derived from the three research questions and translated into user stories representing the perspective of a Professional-level Civil Service Examination aspirant. The following user stories define the MVP scope:

As a user, I want to take a diagnostic pre-test so that the system can identify my strengths and weaknesses before my study sessions begin.
As a user, I want to receive daily adaptive quizzes that focus on my weak areas so that my study time is directed toward the topics I need most.
As a user, I want to earn XP and level up as I complete quizzes so that I feel motivated to continue reviewing.
As a user, I want to receive an AI-generated explanation when I answer a question incorrectly so that I understand my mistake and learn the correct reasoning.
As a user, I want to use an AI chatbox to ask follow-up questions about topics so that I can get clarification without leaving the platform.
As a user, I want to review my previously wrong answers so that I can reinforce my understanding of difficult items.
As a user, I want to unlock badges and achievements so that my consistency and progress are recognized.
As a user, I want to take timed quizzes so that I can practice managing my time under exam conditions.
As a user, I want to view my progress statistics and accuracy per subject so that I can track my improvement over time.
As a user, I want content organized into category-based lessons so that I can navigate the material in a structured way.
As a user, I want certain question sets to be cached locally so that I can continue reviewing even when my internet connection is temporarily unavailable.

Features deferred to future development include Sub-professional level content and mobile browser optimization.

Sprint Structure
CiviQuest development was organized into six sprints running from December 2025 through the first week of May 2026, with each sprint lasting approximately two weeks. The sprint structure and goals were as follows:

Sprint 0 (December 2025): Repository setup, Visual Studio Code project configuration, Supabase integration, database schema design, and GitFlow branch structure. Goal: establish the full development infrastructure before implementation begins.
Sprint 1 (January 2026, weeks 1–2): User authentication via Supabase Auth, diagnostic pre-test module, and baseline performance data storage. Goal: deliver a working login system and functional pre-test.
Sprint 2 (January 2026, weeks 3–4 to February 2026): Adaptive quiz engine with weak area prioritization, XP calculation, leveling logic, badge unlocks, and streak tracking. Goal: deliver the core quiz and gamification layer.
Sprint 3 (February 2026 to March 2026): DeepSeek AI API integration for wrong answer explanations, AI chatbox module, and wrong answer review feature. Goal: deliver the full AI-assisted learning experience.
Sprint 4 (March 2026 to April 2026): Progress tracking and statistics dashboard, timed quiz mode, category-based lesson navigation, and offline question caching. Goal: complete all remaining MVP features and refine the UI.
Sprint 5 (First week of May 2026): Final testing, bug fixes, performance review, and production release. Goal: deliver a stable and fully tested MVP ready for the validation study.

All sprint planning, task tracking, and release management were conducted through the GitHub Project board under the mc-ccs-batch-25-26/system-repository-team-o repository.

Definition of Done
A backlog item in CiviQuest was considered complete only when all of the following criteria were satisfied:

The feature is fully implemented and functional in the browser as described in the corresponding user story.
The code is committed to the feature branch and merged into the develop branch through a reviewed pull request in the system repository.
Unit tests for the core logic of the feature are written and passing.
The GitHub Actions CI pipeline passes without errors on the pull request before merge.
The relevant section of the manuscript or technical documentation is updated to reflect the completed feature.

System Architecture
CiviQuest was developed as a web-based application using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript as the core front-end technologies, with Visual Studio Code as the primary development environment. The system followed a client-server architecture in which the browser-rendered front end served as the client and Supabase served as the cloud-based backend and database layer. Supabase provided a PostgreSQL database, authentication services, and a RESTful API that the front end communicated with over HTTPS. The DeepSeek AI API was integrated as an external service to power the AI explanation and chatbox features.
The application was organized into the following major components: the User Authentication Module, which handled user registration and login through Supabase Auth; the Pre-Test Module, which administered the diagnostic assessment and stored baseline performance data in Supabase; the Adaptive Quiz Engine, which selected and delivered questions based on the user’s performance history and identified weak areas; the Gamification Module, which managed XP calculation, leveling logic, badge unlocks, and streak tracking; the AI Explanation Module, which sent incorrect answer data to the DeepSeek AI API and returned contextual explanations to the user; the AI Chatbox Module, which allowed users to submit topic-related questions to the DeepSeek AI API and receive responses within the platform; the Progress Tracking Module, which queried Supabase performance records and rendered accuracy statistics and improvement data; and the Offline Caching Module, which stored question sets in the browser cache to support quiz access during temporary internet disruptions.
The user interface was designed for desktop browsers with simplicity and accessibility as primary principles. Pages were kept uncluttered to minimize cognitive load during study sessions, and navigation was structured to allow users to move between modules with minimal clicks. The visual design followed modern web conventions using a clean and readable layout.


03-chapter-3-methods / f-logic-flow.md
――――――――――――――――――――
Logic Flow
The core workflow of CiviQuest follows a structured sequence that begins when a user accesses the platform through a desktop browser and proceeds through login, pre-test, adaptive quiz delivery, AI-assisted feedback, and gamification update.
First, the user registers or logs in through the User Authentication Module, which verifies credentials against Supabase Auth. Upon successful authentication, the system checks whether the user has completed the diagnostic pre-test. If the pre-test has not been completed, the user is routed to the Pre-Test Module, which presents a fixed set of questions covering Verbal Ability, Quantitative Ability, and Logical Reasoning. The results are stored in the Supabase performance table and used to establish the user’s baseline knowledge profile.
Once the pre-test is complete, the Adaptive Quiz Engine queries the user’s performance records to identify subject areas with the lowest accuracy rates. It selects questions from those categories at a higher frequency, ensuring that each daily quiz session prioritizes the user’s weakest areas. The quiz is delivered through the browser interface with an optional timed mode that simulates real examination conditions.
After each answer is submitted, the system evaluates it against the stored correct answer. If the answer is correct, the result is recorded and the user proceeds to the next question. If the answer is incorrect, the AI Explanation Module sends the question, the selected answer, and the correct answer to the DeepSeek AI API, which returns a contextual explanation. This explanation is displayed to the user immediately, promoting understanding over memorization. Users may also open the AI Chatbox at any point to ask follow-up questions about the topic.
At the end of each quiz session, the Gamification Module updates the user’s XP, checks for level-up conditions, evaluates badge unlock criteria, and updates the streak counter. All results are written back to Supabase. The Progress Tracking Module then makes updated statistics available on the user’s dashboard, reflecting the latest accuracy rates, improvement trends, and readiness level.

Data Schema
CiviQuest uses a PostgreSQL database hosted on Supabase. The schema is organized around eight primary tables that support the core features of the system.

users: Stores user account information including id (uuid, primary key), email (unique), username, xp (integer), level (integer), pretest_done (boolean), and created_at (timestamp). This table is managed by Supabase Auth and extended with application-specific fields.
categories: Stores the subject categories covered by the examination including id (uuid, primary key), name (e.g., Verbal Ability, Quantitative Ability, Logical Reasoning), and description.
questions: Stores all quiz questions including id (uuid, primary key), category_id (foreign key referencing categories), question_text, option_a through option_d, correct_answer, and difficulty level. Each question belongs to one category.
quiz_sessions: Records each quiz session taken by a user including id (uuid, primary key), user_id (foreign key referencing users), started_at (timestamp), ended_at (timestamp), score (integer), is_pretest (boolean), and is_timed (boolean). Each session belongs to one user.
quiz_session_answers: Records each individual answer submitted during a quiz session including id (uuid, primary key), session_id (foreign key referencing quiz_sessions), question_id (foreign key referencing questions), selected_answer, and is_correct (boolean). Each record belongs to one session and references one question.
performance: Stores aggregated accuracy data per user per category including id (uuid, primary key), user_id (foreign key referencing users), category_id (foreign key referencing categories), accuracy_rate (float), total_answered (integer), total_correct (integer), and updated_at (timestamp). This table is updated after each quiz session and is the primary input for the adaptive quiz engine.
achievements: Defines the available badges and achievements including id (uuid, primary key), name, description, and trigger_condition (the rule that must be met to unlock the achievement).
user_achievements: Records which achievements each user has unlocked including id (uuid, primary key), user_id (foreign key referencing users), achievement_id (foreign key referencing achievements), and unlocked_at (timestamp).

The performance table is the central data structure supporting the adaptive quiz engine. By querying accuracy rates per category for a given user, the engine determines which subject areas to prioritize in the next quiz session. The quiz_session_answers table supports the wrong answer review feature by providing a complete record of all incorrect responses that can be retrieved and displayed to the user on demand.

Validation Plan
The effectiveness of CiviQuest was evaluated through a comparative study involving twenty participants recruited from the target population of Professional-level Civil Service Examination aspirants. Participants were randomly assigned to one of two groups of ten: a control group and an experimental group.
The control group did not use CiviQuest during the study period. These participants reviewed for the Civil Service Examination using their own preferred methods and materials, representing the current baseline condition of self-directed review without the proposed system. At the end of the study period, all ten control group participants completed a standardized mock Civil Service Examination prepared by the research team.
The experimental group was given full access to CiviQuest through their desktop browsers for the same study period. These participants used the web application as their primary review tool, completing the diagnostic pre-test on their first session, engaging with daily adaptive quizzes, and utilizing the DeepSeek AI-powered explanation and chatbox features throughout the review period. At the end of the study period, all ten experimental group participants completed the same standardized mock Civil Service Examination administered to the control group.
The primary metric used to evaluate system effectiveness was the mock examination pass rate of each group. A higher pass rate in the experimental group relative to the control group was interpreted as evidence that CiviQuest improved examination readiness compared to unguided self-directed review. Secondary metrics included subject-specific accuracy scores and improvement rates derived from in-application performance data recorded in the Supabase database throughout the study period for the experimental group.
In addition to performance metrics, a usability evaluation was conducted with the experimental group following the study period. Participants completed a structured questionnaire assessing the usability of the web interface, the usefulness of individual features, and their overall satisfaction with the platform. The combined results of the comparative performance study and the usability evaluation provided the evidence base for addressing RQ3 and for identifying areas for improvement in future development iterations.
